# Customer segments

Access date for the evidence cited here: 24 October 2026. Scores are judgements. They are not measurements. Each score is 1 to 5. A 5 means more attractive to a solo founder. Uncertain scores are marked. Changing the weights does not create an unconditional decision to build a general school ERP.

Related documents: [market-analysis.md](market-analysis.md), [competitor-matrix.md](competitor-matrix.md), [product-positioning.md](product-positioning.md).

## 1. Weights

| Criterion | Weight | What a high score means |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Market demand and accessibility | 12% | There are enough reachable buyers, not merely a large census count |
| Severity of the customer problem | 12% | The pain is frequent and operational, not a vague wish for “digital transformation” |
| Willingness to pay | 14% | Published prices or other evidence show money already moving |
| Competitive intensity | 10% | 5 means easier entry. 1 means many adequate alternatives |
| Solo-developer feasibility | 14% | One person can design, build, and sell the first version |
| Product differentiation | 8% | A specific reason to choose a new product exists, beyond a new framework |
| Support and customization burden | 12% | 5 means a light, repeatable support load |
| Expansion potential | 8% | A second segment or country can reuse the same core |
| CodeCanyon suitability | 4% | Buyers on that marketplace would choose this segment’s product |
| SaaS suitability | 6% | A monthly hosted product fits how the buyer purchases |
| Total | 100% | |

CodeCanyon suitability is deliberately light. The marketplace evidence says a new script is a weak business, so a segment should not win because it would make a good Envato listing.

The weighted total is the sum of `score × weight`, divided by 5, expressed out of 100. A score of 5 on every row would be 100.

## 3. Why each score was given

### Coaching centres and tuition academies

Demand is 3 and uncertain because no census of centres was found. Supply-side evidence is real: [CampusQ](https://campusqbd.com/pricing), [ClassTablet](https://classtablet.com/en-US/coaching-center-software), and [Apps Maker BD](https://appsmakerbd.com/services/coaching-center-management-system/) sell to this buyer. Severity is 4 because the published products are organised around batches, monthly fees, attendance, and parent messages, which is a sign that those jobs are the product. That is still an inference from vendor menus, not from interviews. Willingness to pay is only 2: the visible prices are BDT 499–2,499 per month or about BDT 20 per student. Competition is 2 because those offers already exist. Solo feasibility is 5 because a batch, a due list, and attendance are a coherent product. Differentiation is 3 and uncertain: a clearer dues screen is a plausible edge, and it is not yet proven against CampusQ. Support burden is 4 because board marksheets, hostels, and multi-campus accounting are out of scope. SaaS fit is 5 because the existing offers are already monthly.

### Independent private schools and kindergartens

The 2023 census, via Bonik Barta, reports 26,461 kindergartens. That supports a demand score of 4, with uncertainty because 2023 is not 2026 and most of those schools may never pay. Problem severity is 4 as an inference from the fact that Pathshala, EduZone, MicroCampus, and Aara all sell fee collection and attendance into this population. Willingness to pay is 3 because prices are published and low, not because a study of conversion exists. Competition is 2. Solo feasibility is 3: one campus is buildable until the school asks for board report cards and on-site training. Differentiation is 2. A new general ERP does not have a sharp contrast with Pathshala at BDT 500–1,200 a month.

### English-medium and international schools in Bangladesh

Demand is 2 because Prothom Alo and bdnews24 both report about 143 English-medium schools, despite disagreeing on enrolment. Willingness to pay is 4 and uncertain: higher tuition makes a software budget plausible, and no fee schedule for school software aimed at this segment was found. Solo feasibility and support are 2 because Cambridge-style reporting and parent expectations are a different product. Expansion is 4 only in the sense that the same curriculum exists in other cities. That is not permission to build it first. CodeCanyon fit is 1. Those buyers do not purchase a $67 PHP script and upload it to cPanel as their operating system. **Inference.**

### Bangla-medium secondary schools

The institution count is large, 19,211 secondary schools in the Prothom Alo citation of BANBEIS 2025, so this is not a tiny segment. Accessibility of a paying buyer is the constraint, so demand is 3 rather than 5. Many of these schools have committees, board obligations, and weak administrative budgets. Willingness to pay is 2. Support burden is 1 because tabulation, Bangla marksheets, and local political or committee decision-making do not scale for one developer. This score will look wrong to anyone who equates “most schools” with “best customer”. The census and the support load are different questions.

### Madrasas

Institution count is large, 9,296 in the same BANBEIS news report, excluding Ebtedayee and Qawmi. Demand is still 3 because reach and budget were not observed. Competition is 3 and uncertain: fewer vendors advertise a madrasa-only product, while general ERPs mention madrasah as a checkbox. Differentiation could exist for Dakhil grading and residential operations. Support burden is 1 until the founder has operated that exam system. Building a madrasa product from a feature matrix would be speculation.

### Multi-branch groups

Willingness to pay is 4 because MicroCampus publishes a BDT 120,000+ tier and Pathshala explicitly refuses to let one licence cover a second branch. The count of such groups is unknown, so demand is 2. Solo feasibility and support are 1. The second campus is where permissions, consolidated receipts, and “our other branch does it differently” begin. CodeCanyon fit is 3 only because eSchool SaaS has about 1,003 sales selling a multi-school kit to operators. Those buyers are software entrepreneurs, not the school group itself.

## 4. Weight sensitivity

Base rank: coaching 67.2, private kindergartens 59.2, multi-branch 52.4, English-medium 51.6, madrasas 45.2, Bangla-medium 43.6.

Alternative weights, still summing to 100, that take willingness to pay more seriously and solo feasibility less seriously:

| Criterion | Base | Alternative |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Demand and accessibility | 12 | 10 |
| Problem severity | 12 | 10 |
| Willingness to pay | 14 | 24 |
| Competitive intensity | 10 | 12 |
| Solo feasibility | 14 | 6 |
| Differentiation | 8 | 8 |
| Support burden | 12 | 8 |
| Expansion | 8 | 8 |
| CodeCanyon fit | 4 | 4 |
| SaaS fit | 6 | 10 |

Alternative totals:

| Segment | Total |
| --- | ---: |
| Coaching centres | 62.0 |
| Private kindergartens and small private schools | 59.6 |
| Multi-branch groups | 59.2 |
| English-medium | 56.0 |
| Madrasas | 45.6 |
| Bangla-medium secondary | 44.4 |

Coaching still leads. Private schools and multi-branch groups move up because they score higher on willingness to pay. None of them crosses into a comfortable build. The alternative weighting does not rescue a general ERP, and it does not make English-medium schools a first market. If interviews show coaching willingness to pay at 1 rather than 2, the base coaching total falls by `(1×14) / 5 = 2.8` points for each point removed, to 64.4 if willingness is 1. Private schools would still not overtake it unless coaching’s solo and support scores were also wrong.

**Reading.** The ranking is stable enough to pick a beachhead. It is not stable enough to skip interviews. The uncertain scores are demand, severity, and differentiation for coaching, and almost the whole English-medium willingness story.

## 5. Choice

| Decision | Choice | Why the others wait |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary segment | Coaching centres and tuition academies in Dhaka | Highest weighted score. One workflow. The owner can say yes |
| Initial geography | Dhaka | The founder can visit. National rollout adds language, training, and connectivity problems before the product is proven. English-medium schools are also concentrated in cities, and there are still too few of them |
| Buying persona | The centre owner who also watches the bKash inbox and the due list | Teachers feel attendance pain and usually cannot approve a subscription. An accountant may operate the product and still needs the owner to pay |
| Primary problem | The centre cannot reliably say who has paid, who paid in part, and which guardian still owes, without reconstructing a register and a personal wallet history | Exams, payroll, and a parent super-app are real requests and poor first products |
| Launch model | Hosted SaaS in BDT | CodeCanyon buyers are script customers. Dhaka owners are not. See [pricing-and-unit-economics.md](pricing-and-unit-economics.md) |
| Second expansion | Small urban private kindergartens, only if they will use the same fee and attendance core | They scored second. They pull the product toward marksheets and annual calendars. That pull is allowed only after coaching pilots are paying |

### Deferred on purpose

- **Bangla-medium secondary schools.** Largest familiar label, worst support score. Board report cards are a product of their own.
- **Madrasas.** Large, specialised, and unproven as a paying software market in the sources reviewed.
- **English-medium schools.** About 143 institutions in the news reports, high expectations, and global SIS alternatives.
- **Multi-branch groups.** Better invoices, a project business rather than a product business.
- **CodeCanyon agencies.** They buy source code and then demand install help. That is a services funnel, and the author fee is 50%.

International expansion is not a segment choice for this phase. It is recorded as a hypothesis in the market analysis and rejected as a launch plan.
