## 5. What buyers appear to reward

This section is **inference** from sales, prices, and listing text. It is not a survey.

| Buyer priority | Evidence | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feature coverage | Every high-sale listing advertises admissions, attendance, exams, fees, and then library, transport, and hostel | A script that omits the familiar menu is harder to sell on CodeCanyon. That does not mean a Dhaka coaching centre needs the whole menu |
| Price in a narrow band | Established scripts cluster at $50–$89. Mighty School at $29 has 66 sales. Samyotech at $99 has 3 | A premium CodeCanyon price is not justified by stack alone |
| Install path | Inilabs documents cPanel upload and a purchase code. Samyotech warns that shared hosting will fail | CodeCanyon buyers still expect PHP hosting. A Next.js and PostgreSQL product breaks that expectation |
| Demo | Live preview links are standard on the large listings | A buyer can click a demo before paying $50. Demo quality itself was not scored |
| Support, grudgingly | Every support tab found offers 6 months and then excludes installation and customization. InfixEdu says the quiet part aloud | Buyers ask for install help. Authors try not to include it. A new author who offers unlimited customization will be consumed by it |
| Technology brand | The only captured Node/TypeScript school ERP has 3 sales. Laravel Ekattor 8, from a famous brand, has hundreds of sales, not thousands | Current stack is not the purchase reason |
| Ongoing vendor viability | Smart School, InfixEdu, eSchool, and Ekattor 8 were all updated in 2026 | Dead products are a known fear. It is still not evidence that buyers are happy |

High sales are not satisfaction. Smart School can be both widely purchased and painful to customize. This research did not read the review text, so it does not claim that buyers are delighted or angry. The support policies are the usable public signal: installation and customization are where the cost sits, and authors push that cost back to the buyer.

## 6. CodeCanyon questions

**Is the category saturated, growing, or hard to enter?**

Cumulative sales of the older PHP products are large: roughly 6,100, 3,800, 2,400, and 1,500 on the four leaders. Products launched or refreshed in the last one to four years do not show that trajectory, apart from eSchool SaaS, which sells a Flutter-plus-Laravel package to people building a multi-school business and still took about three years to pass 1,000 sales. Category growth rate is **Unknown**. Difficulty of entry is an **inference**: high, because feature expectations are already set by incumbents and new listings are not accumulating similar sales.

**Apparent price range.** $29 to $99 on the items captured, with the commercial centre at $50–$89. Add-ons are $19–$65, and bundles reach $199. Direct-store premium tiers, such as InstiKit at $299, are outside the usual CodeCanyon tag.

**Minimum feature coverage buyers expect.** From the overlap of the high-sale listings, a CodeCanyon buyer expects student records, class and section setup, attendance, exams and report cards, fee collection and dues, a parent or student role, notices, and a pile of “also included” modules such as library, transport, and hostel. Coaching-only scope would look incomplete on that marketplace even if it is the right product in Dhaka.

**Overrepresented features.** Library, hostel, transport, live class (Zoom, Meet, Jitsi, BigBlueButton), multi-language flags, and generic payroll. They are present because they photograph well in a feature grid. There is no public evidence in this research that they are the reason a school renews.

**Repeated complaints.** Specific complaint text was **not verified** from CodeCanyon reviews. What is verified is the boundary authors draw: no free customization, often no installation, refunds refused after download, one school per licence in at least one listing, and paid work for a new payment gateway. That pattern is the complaint waiting to happen.

**What a solo developer can support.** A hosted product with one workflow, office-hour replies, and no on-site training. Not a CodeCanyon item that must keep parity with Smart School’s add-on list, answer install tickets, and ship PHP updates for years.

**Is a one-time licence viable after fees, updates, and support?** Only at incumbent volume, which a new item should not assume. From 1 July 2026 the author fee is 50% of the item-price component ([author hub](https://author.envato.com/hub/changes-to-envato-market-revenue-share-and-exclusivity-what-you-need-to-know/), [earnings help](https://help.author.envato.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000472943-Introduction-to-Earnings)). Illustrative arithmetic is in [pricing-and-unit-economics.md](pricing-and-unit-economics.md). If each sale creates even a few hours of install or bug work, a $79 item can fail to pay for that work.

**Would a premium price be justified?** Not on CodeCanyon, on this evidence. The $99 Node listing is the premium experiment, and it has 3 sales. A higher price would need a different channel and a buyer who is purchasing an outcome, not a script.

**Licensing restrictions to check before any marketplace launch.**

- Envato licenses the item to the buyer. Regular licence: the end product is available free of charge. Extended licence: the end product is sold or restricted to paying users. One licence, one end product ([licence FAQ](https://help.market.envato.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005593363-Do-I-need-a-Regular-License-or-an-Extended-License)).
- A school that installs a script and lets parents use it without paying the software vendor is the usual regular-licence case. An agency that hosts the same purchase for many paying schools is likely to need a licence per end product. That reading should be confirmed against the current licence text before anyone sells implementation services around a CodeCanyon script.
- If this founder later sold the script on CodeCanyon, exclusivity would not block a direct SaaS, because exclusivity ended on 1 July 2026. The 50% fee would still apply to marketplace sales.
- InfixEdu’s own “one school” sentence shows that authors add rules. Any future listing needs its own plain-language licence, written before launch, not copied from a competitor.

## 7. Does Next.js, TypeScript, and PostgreSQL win?

**No, not as a CodeCanyon differentiator.** The verified counterexample is the Samyotech listing: TypeScript, React, Node, $99, 3 sales, and an explicit statement that shared hosting will not work. The market that buys downloadable school scripts still runs PHP. PostgreSQL is a good database for a product the founder hosts. It is a deployment obstacle for a buyer who expects phpMyAdmin on a $5 host.

The stack can still be the right engineering choice for a hosted SaaS that the founder operates. That advantage is reliability, typed financial code, and not inheriting a decade of CodeIgniter patches. It is an advantage for the operator, not a reason for the buyer to pay more. The buyer in Dhaka is comparing Pathshala at a few hundred taka a month and a paper register, not comparing ORMs.
