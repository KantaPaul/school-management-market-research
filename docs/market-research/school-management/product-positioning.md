# Product positioning

Access date for competitor evidence: 24 October 2026. Positioning statements below are proposals to test. They are not claims the product may already make in advertising. Several depend on interviews that have not been done.

## 1. Three alternatives

### A. Finance-first administration for coaching centres

For a Dhaka coaching-centre owner who cannot tell which students have actually paid, this product shows today’s dues, partial payments, and receipts in one list, unlike a general school ERP or a paper register, because it is limited to batches, attendance, and fee collection in Bangla and English and does not require a software installer.

### B. Simple, modern school ERP for small private schools

For the owner of a small private school who is tired of scattered registers, this product runs admission, attendance, exams, and fees in one hosted system, unlike a CodeCanyon PHP script, because it is operated for the school and does not ask the owner to manage a server.

### C. Bangladesh-localised school management with international expansion potential

For Bangladeshi institutions that want local payments and Bangla records now and foreign campuses later, this product is one school system for many countries, unlike local vendors who only sell in Bangladesh and unlike iSAMS or Veracross, because it starts local and is built to be extended.

## 2. Comparison

| Test | A. Finance-first coaching | B. Simple school ERP | C. Local now, international later |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Matches the segment score | Yes. Coaching scored 67.2 | Partially. Private schools scored 59.2 and need a wider product | No. International launch was rejected |
| Buyer can understand it in one sentence | Yes. Unpaid students and receipts | Weak. “Simple ERP” is what every vendor says | Weak. The buyer in Dhaka is not purchasing a future country |
| Incumbents | CampusQ, ClassTablet, paper, personal bKash | Pathshala, EduZone, MicroCampus, Aara, SchoolSoftBD, CodeCanyon scripts | All of the above, plus QuickSchools, Sycamore, iSAMS, Veracross |
| What must be true | Owners lose time or money reconciling dues and will pay monthly | Schools will switch to another general system for a modest monthly fee | A second country will pay for abstractions built early |
| Evidence today | Vendors already sell this narrower job. The pain itself is not yet interviewed | Vendors already sell the broad job at low prices. “Modern stack” did not sell on CodeCanyon | No Nepal or Sri Lanka research. US and UK products are a different service business |
| Solo support load | Lowest of the three | Marksheets, promotion, and parent complaints expand the load | Localization and compliance expand it again |
| Failure mode | The price ceiling is BDT 499–2,499 and the product is too small to matter | The founder rebuilds Smart School and still loses on features | The founder builds a platform for customers who do not exist yet |

## 3. Selected position

**Use A.** B is the product the brief hoped to build, and the competitor set already occupies it at a lower price and with more modules. C is an ambition. It makes the first release slower without creating a buyer.

A is allowed to expand toward B later, for small kindergartens, only if those schools accept batch-style fees and attendance and do not require a grading engine to sign a pilot. C is not a roadmap item until one geography renews.

### Ideal customer profile

- One coaching centre or tuition academy.
- In Dhaka, so the founder can sit with the owner.
- Roughly 40 to 250 active students. Below that, a notebook may be rational. Above that, the centre starts to want branches, payroll, and a custom app.
- Fees are collected monthly, in cash and by bKash or Nagad, often as partial payments.
- The owner is present. There is no IT staff.
- Current system is a register, a spreadsheet, a personal wallet, or a cheap tool the owner already finds inadequate.

The student-count band is a **hypothesis**. No census of centre size was found.

### Primary buyer

The owner. This person feels missing fees as personal cash. They can approve BDT 1,000–4,000 a month without a committee. If a centre has a managing partner group, the buyer is the partner who reconciles collections.

### Daily users

- The owner, in the evening, checking dues.
- A clerk or accountant, recording receipts during the day.
- A teacher, marking attendance for a batch in under a few minutes.

Guardians are not daily users of an admin system. They receive a receipt or a message. A login-based guardian portal is optional and later.

### Main buying trigger

A concrete money event: a month in which the owner could not say how much was still unpaid, a guardian who claimed to have paid, a teacher who collected cash that never reached the register, or a decision to open another batch and the current sheet no longer fits. “We should modernise” is a weak trigger and should not be counted as one in interviews.

### Current alternatives

- Paper register and a personal bKash account.
- A spreadsheet.
- [CampusQ](https://campusqbd.com/pricing) at up to BDT 2,499 per month.
- [ClassTablet](https://classtablet.com/en-US/coaching-center-software) at a vendor-stated BDT 20 per student.
- A one-off build such as [Apps Maker BD](https://appsmakerbd.com/services/coaching-center-management-system/) from BDT 45,000.
- A general school package such as [Pathshala](https://pathshalasms.com/) if the centre is willing to live inside a school menu.

### Core promise

At the end of the day the owner can see, for each batch, who paid, who paid part, who owes, and what receipt was issued. Attendance for that batch is recorded by the teacher without a separate system.

### Reasons to believe

These are requirements for the product, not achievements.

- The screen is a due list, not a 40-module menu.
- Partial payments are first-class. The balance is the invoice minus recorded payments, in integer poisha, with an audit row for each change.
- Bangla and English are both available for the receipt and the due list.
- The centre can export its students, invoices, and payments to a spreadsheet.
- The founder hosts it. The owner does not buy a server or a licence key.
- A pilot can start with one batch, which is the same adoption step ClassTablet already describes. Matching a competitor’s onboarding speed is table stakes.

### Reasons a customer might reject it

- CampusQ or ClassTablet is already good enough and cheaper.
- The owner wants the software on their own PC and does not trust a hosted system.
- They want a branded Android app in the first month.
- They want live bKash settlement into their bank before they will record a payment. That integration is not in the first release, and merchant approval is not guaranteed.
- They ask for payroll, hostel, or board marksheets and will not pay without them.
- A nephew or a local shop will customise a CodeCanyon script for a one-time fee.

### Positioning risks

| Risk | Why it is plausible |
| --- | --- |
| The pain is real and still not worth BDT 1,999 | Published coaching prices already start at BDT 499 |
| “Finance-first” sounds like accounting software | Owners may expect a cash book and payroll, which are out of scope |
| Coaching centres are too heterogeneous | An English-medium admission-test centre and a neighbourhood tutoring room may not share a workflow. Interviews have to show the same due-list job |
| The second segment captures the roadmap | Kindergarten owners will ask for report cards. Saying yes turns the product into B |
| The phrase “unlike a general school ERP” is easy to betray | Adding library and transport in the first year makes the sentence false |

### Evidence needed before this positioning is used in a sale

- At least eight coaching owners, interviewed with the script in [customer-validation-plan.md](customer-validation-plan.md), describe the same reconciliation failure without being led to it.
- At least two of them state a current monthly cost or a recent loss, and agree to pay for a pilot on one batch.
- At least one owner who already uses CampusQ, ClassTablet, Pathshala, or a developer explains the specific missing behaviour, not “the software is bad”.
- A price conversation that includes BDT 999, 1,999, and 3,999, recorded before the founder argues for a tier.
- A written list of requested features. If payroll, hostel, transport, or custom board cards dominate, this position is wrong.

Until those notes exist, the positioning statement is a research conclusion, not a slogan for a landing page.
