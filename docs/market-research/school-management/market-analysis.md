# Market analysis

Access date: 24 October 2026. This document separates Bangladesh from international markets and labels every material claim. Competitor prices and marketplace sales are tabulated in [competitor-matrix.md](competitor-matrix.md). The source list is [sources.md](sources.md).

## 1. How to read the numbers

Bangladesh has a large number of educational institutions. That is not a count of buyers. A government primary school, an MPO-funded secondary school, a Dhaka English-medium school, a Qawmi madrasa, and a coaching centre do not share a budget, a decision-maker, or a grading system. This research does not estimate a taka total addressable market. The inputs required for that estimate, including the share of institutions that pay for software and the price they actually pay, were not observed.

## 2. Bangladesh institution landscape

### Secondary schools

Prothom Alo, citing BANBEIS Bangladesh Education Statistics 2025, reports 19,211 government and private secondary schools including junior schools, with more than 7.439 million students. In 2024 the same series was 19,117 schools and 7,847,546 students. School-and-college institutions rose from 1,514 to 1,556. The article also says secondary-level enrolment overall fell by more than 400,000 in one year. Source: [Prothom Alo, English](https://en.prothomalo.com/youth/education/f8t5oc4rxz) and [Bangla](https://www.prothomalo.com/education/gyeib3qecm). Evidence type: **verified news report of an official statistic**, not a figure re-read from the BANBEIS PDF.

**Inference.** A shrinking enrolment base makes per-student software pricing less attractive to a school owner, and it makes “the market is growing because there are millions of students” an unsafe pitch.

### Madrasas

The same Prothom Alo report: 9,296 madrasas offering Dakhil, Alim, Fazil, and Kamil, with more than 2.84 million students in 2025, up from 9,269 institutions and more than 2.796 million students in 2024. Independent Ebtedayee and Qawmi madrasas are excluded, so the religious-education total is larger than this table. **Verified as a news citation. Unknown** how many of these institutions have a software budget.

### English-medium schools

Both Prothom Alo articles report 143 English-medium schools. They report 2025 enrolment of 29,672, up from 29,077. bdnews24, also citing BANBEIS, reports a different series: 68,825 students in 2021, rising to 79,589 in 2025, and 143 schools in 2025 up from 123 in 2021, with 141 of 143 in city corporations. Sources: [Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/youth/education/f8t5oc4rxz), [bdnews24](https://bdnews.mobile/news/english-medium-schools-bangladesh-fees-student-growth-d6aa09).

**Do not reconcile these enrolment figures.** The school count of 143 is the more stable number across the two reports and is still a very small set of institutions. Even if every one of them paid, a solo founder would be selling into a few dozen realistic accounts, not a national market. Curriculum, fees, and parent expectations inside that set are **not verified** school by school.

### Primary, kindergarten, and government schools

Bonik Barta, citing the Annual Primary School Census 2023, reports 114,630 primary-level institutions, 65,567 government primary schools, 6,134 private primary schools, and 26,461 kindergartens with 4,873,375 students. English-medium and Qawmi institutions were outside that census. Source: [Bonik Barta](https://en.bonikbarta.com/bangladesh/6DXJhWSnUkgMo5fU). The Directorate of Primary Education’s APSS 2024 divisional file reports 118,607 institutions covered and 24,308 in Dhaka division. Source: [APSS 2024 PDF](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-dpe/2024/12/a0f9332e7073419090e529f103bc902d.pdf).

**Inference.** Government primary schools are not the customer. The kindergarten count is the nearest thing to a large private-school population, and it is a 2023 figure reported by a newspaper. How many of those kindergartens collect meaningful tuition, are in Dhaka, and will pay a monthly software fee is **Unknown**.

### Coaching centres

**Unknown count.** No official census was found. Vendors behave as if the segment exists: CampusQ prices batches and courses, ClassTablet prices coaching centres in taka per student, and Apps Maker BD sells a fixed-price coaching build. That is evidence of supply, not of market size.

The strongest combination of pain, ability to pay, access to the buyer, limited customization, a short cycle, and a support load one person can carry is **not** “all private schools”. It is the coaching-centre fee desk, with small urban kindergartens as a later adjacent buyer only if they accept the same narrow product. The scoring is in [customer-segments.md](customer-segments.md). Willingness to pay is the weak point even in the winning segment: the published prices are low.

## 5. Fees, language, and the mobile reality

Vendors who publish a Bangladesh offer almost all mention the same daily jobs:

- Monthly or term fees, receipts, and outstanding balances.
- Discounts and partial payments, claimed by MicroCampus and visible in the general ERP feature lists. How partial payments are accounted for was **not verified** by using the products.
- Attendance, often tied to an SMS to the guardian.
- Exam results or marksheets. Bangla-medium and madrasa products advertise board-style tabulation. Coaching products advertise batch results.
- Bengali interfaces. ClassTablet states that the interface, notices, and reports are in Bengali. Pathshala’s price table is written in Bangla. Other vendors mix English marketing pages with a local product.

**Inference, not a surveyed gap.** Guardians often use a phone and a wallet more readily than a parent portal. Several vendors lead with SMS rather than with an app. A product that assumes an email login and a desktop report will miss the actual payer.

Internet reliability and shared office PCs were **not measured**. Biometric vendors (MicroCampus) advertise an offline hardware buffer because connectivity fails. That is a vendor claim and a warning: live wallet checkout and live SMS are fragile if they are on the critical path of marking a student present.

| Integration | What is verified | What is not |

| Nagad direct API | **Not verified** from an official Nagad developer page | Current credentials, eligibility, and fees. A 2021 PDF on a document host was not treated as policy |

**Product consequence.** The first release should store cash, bKash, and Nagad as recorded payments with a transaction reference and a receipt. It should not block a centre’s fee desk on a live API. Live checkout is a later project that starts only after a merchant agreement exists. School-level “payment link” products that sweep into the founder’s own wallet create a custody problem this research does not need to solve in version one.

## 7. Data protection

The Personal Data Protection Ordinance, 2025 is repealed. The Personal Data Protection Act, 2026, Act No. 63, dated 10 April 2026, is the statute. Section 44 repeals the 2025 ordinance and the 2026 amendment ordinance. Source: [Act](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-details-1692.html), [section 44](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1692/section-print-57310.html).

Relevant verified provisions, paraphrased from the Bangla text, not a legal opinion:

- A child is a person under 18, or a lower age if the government sets one.
- Processing a child’s personal data requires consent from a parent, legal guardian, or another person authorised to decide.
- Consent must be voluntary, specific, informed, and withdrawable. The controller has to be able to prove it.
- Personal data is not to be disclosed for a new purpose without consent, subject to the rest of the Act.
- Sections 23 and 31–35 do not take effect until a gazette after 18 months from enactment. Other sections are deemed effective from 6 November 2025.

This research does **not** say the product is compliant. It says a school or coaching SaaS that stores children’s names, guardians’ phone numbers, attendance, and payment references is inside the subject matter of the Act. Counsel should read the Act before any pilot stores real student data. Design implications that do not require a compliance claim: collect fewer fields, record who consented and when, keep a deletion path, and do not add advertising or behavioural profiling of students.

## 8. How a new product would have to justify its price

Local alternatives already cover the brochure:

- Under BDT 1,500 a month for a small institution on Pathshala or CampusQ.
- Under BDT 5 per student per month on EduZone’s monthly line.
- A one-time BDT 35,000–65,000 if the buyer hates subscriptions, from MicroCampus or similar offers.
- A downloaded PHP script at $50–$89 if the buyer has a developer.

A new subscription cannot win by listing more modules. Those modules are already on sale. A justifiable reason to pay, which is still a **hypothesis**, is narrower:

- The owner can see, on a phone, which students are unpaid, partly paid, or paid, without matching a bKash inbox to a register.
- Receipts and waivers do not depend on one clerk’s notebook.
- The product is in Bangla, the export belongs to the centre, and the founder does not disappear when the monthly fee is late.
- Setup is done remotely in an afternoon for one batch, which is the implementation story ClassTablet already tells. Matching that story is the minimum, not a differentiator.

If interviews show that owners are content with a register plus bKash, or that they already pay CampusQ or a local developer less than the support cost, the price is not justifiable and the project should stop.

## 9. International markets

“International customers” is not a market. The products found split into at least three groups.

### High-touch independent-school systems

iSAMS, Veracross, and similar systems sell to schools with staff who can run an SIS. iSAMS appears on Software Advice from £6,000 per year. That is a directory listing, not iSAMS’s quote. Veracross publishes no price. Capterra UK shows a $500 per month directory starting price and names Australia, Canada, the United Kingdom, and the United States. Implementation, training, and data migration are part of the sale. A solo developer in Dhaka cannot provide the timezone coverage, procurement paperwork, or on-site-equivalent onboarding these buyers expect. **Inference** from the pricing model and the absence of a self-serve public checkout for Veracross.

### Published per-student systems for smaller schools

QuickSchools publishes $0.99–$2.99 per student per month with a 30-student minimum. Sycamore publishes $4 per student per month and an 18-month term. Gradelink uses a calculator. One captured calculator state was $106 per month plus $275 setup, and the student count behind it was not recorded, so that dollar figure must not be quoted as “the” price.

A flat $29, $79, or $149 per school per month does not match how these vendors price. At QuickSchools’ middle tier of $1.49, a 100-student school is already about $149 per month. At Sycamore’s $4, it is $400. Undercutting them with a new, unproven SIS would still require US or UK support, card payments, and privacy obligations this research did not review. FERPA and UK GDPR were **not** read for this paper. They are named only as examples of obligations that would have to be read before any sale, not as a compliance checklist that has been completed.

### South Asian SaaS already priced like Bangladesh

EduSaaS in Pakistan advertises a free tier up to 100 students and an advance plan at Rs 1,499 per month, with fee vouchers, attendance, and parent access on the vendor site ([edusaas.site](https://edusaas.site/)). Vendor claims of module counts are not audited. The useful fact is that a neighbouring market already has a cheap hosted school product. Entering it later means competing with that price, in another language and payments system, without a local support person.

### Two expansion markets, and why neither is a launch market

**Nepal. Hypothesis only.** The research did not review Nepali school-software vendors, eSewa or Khalti terms, or Nepal’s privacy law. The reason it is even a candidate is structural similarity: private schools and tuition centres, a mobile-wallet habit, and a price level closer to Bangladesh than to iSAMS. Acquisition would require Nepali language support, local payment merchant approval, and a partner or the founder’s own travel. None of that was validated. Do not treat Nepal as an open market.

**Sri Lanka. Hypothesis only.** English is more widely usable in private and international schools than in a mass Bangla-medium market, which reduces one localization cost. The competitive set, the rupee price ceiling, and the personal-data statute were **not reviewed**. A solo founder would still need a local introducer. International-school buyers in Colombo can already look at the same global SIS products as Dhaka English-medium schools.

Markets that are not recommended, on the evidence above: the United States, the United Kingdom, and “international schools” as a single segment. They can pay more, and they require a company this founder does not have.

The prompt’s long-term ambition, one product for many countries and institution types, is a version-two architecture constraint at most. It is not a launch plan. Building the abstractions for madrasas, British grading, and US payments before one Dhaka centre has paid is how the six months disappear.

## 11. Market conclusion

Bangladesh is a real software market with published prices and many institutions. It is also a low-price market with local vendors already selling the broad school ERP. CodeCanyon is a real script market with entrenched PHP products and a 50% author fee. International SIS budgets are real and inaccessible to a solo founder without a local support operation.

The commercial opening, if interviews confirm it, is a hosted fee-and-attendance product for Dhaka coaching centres, priced honestly against CampusQ and ClassTablet, not a multi-country school ERP and not a CodeCanyon script.
